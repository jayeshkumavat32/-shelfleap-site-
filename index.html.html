The problem

Two risks nobody has solved

Thousands of new Indian brands launch every year. Millions of independent stores could stock them. Almost nothing connects the two.

For indie brands

Getting a first meeting with a retailer is slow and unpredictable. There's no standard way to pitch a product, prove it deserves shelf space, or get it in front of stores that actually fit the brand's category, price point and city.

For independent retailers

Shelf space is limited and every unknown product is a bet. Without a way to sample it, see customer reaction and gather feedback first, trying something new means real financial risk on every single order.

Your journey

Seven steps from submission to reorder

1. Register your brand

Brand name, founder, city, website or Instagram, category and SKU count. Takes about five minutes.

2. Submit your product

Product name, type, MRP, wholesale price, MOQ, shelf life, description, images, certifications and sample availability.

3. Get verified

Our team reviews your submission and awards ShelfLeap Verified status — the signal retailers trust before sampling.

4. Send samples

Your product joins a curated monthly Innovation Box that goes out to matched retailers. No individual courier chasing.

5. Receive real feedback

Retailers rate quality, packaging, price and store fit, then say Yes / Maybe / No with expected monthly quantity.

6–7. Orders & reorders

Interested retailers place B2B wholesale orders at your MOQ. Track which stores reorder — that's your real product-market fit signal.

Questions

What retailers usually ask

Does the Innovation Box cost anything?

No. During the pilot, the box is free for selected retailers. We're validating whether sampling converts into real orders before introducing any fee.

Am I obligated to order after sampling?

Not at all. You can say Yes, Maybe or No on every product. A "No" with a reason is just as useful to us and to the brand as an order.

Who decides which products I receive?

Your own preferences do — category, price range and store type. In the prototype stage, every match is also manually approved before a box ships.

What if a product sells badly?

You only bought a sample-tested product at your own chosen quantity, at the brand's MOQ. That's the entire point: the risk moves from your shelf to the sampling stage.

How do payments work?

The retailer pays for a B2B order, ShelfLeap records the transaction and its commission, and the brand receives settlement. During the prototype, checkout is a test flow — no live payment gateway.

How money moves

ShelfLeap is a marketplace facilitator: the retailer pays for the order, the platform records the transaction and its commission, and the brand receives settlement.

Brand → ShelfLeap

Product listing and sample stock for the Innovation Box.

ShelfLeap → Retailer

Curated box, delivered and tracked, free during the pilot.

Retailer → ShelfLeap

Structured feedback, then a B2B wholesale order at MOQ.

ShelfLeap → Brand

Order information and settlement, net of platform commission.

Illustrative only: on a ₹10,000 order, a 10% commission would mean ₹1,000 to the platform and ₹9,000 settled to the brand. The actual commission rate will be validated during the pilot. The prototype uses a test checkout — no live payment gateway.

About ShelfLeap

Shelf space should go to the best product, not the biggest budget.

ShelfLeap exists because India's most interesting new products and its most important retail channel have no reliable way to find each other.

Our vision

A country where any good product can reach a real shelf within 90 days of launch.

We imagine an India where a founder in a micro-production unit in Ahmedabad has the same structured path to retail distribution as a national FMCG brand — and where every independent retailer can stock genuinely new products without gambling on them.

Our mission

Replace cold pitching and blind ordering with verified sampling and real feedback.

We build the trust layer between indie brands and independent retailers: verifying who's real, matching who fits, moving the samples, capturing the feedback and closing the first B2B order — so both sides make decisions on evidence rather than on hope.

What drives us

Five things we hold to

01

Evidence over intuition

Every stocking decision on ShelfLeap should rest on a sample that was actually tested and feedback that was actually given.

02

The retailer's risk comes first

Shelf space is a small retailer's scarcest asset. If we ever make it riskier to use, we've built the wrong product.

03

Verification is the product

Anyone can build a listings page. The value is in knowing that the brand behind the listing is real and the numbers are honest.

04

Start manual, earn the automation

No AI matching, no nationwide marketplace until the human version of the loop demonstrably works in one city.

05

Local before national

Ahmedabad first, properly. A platform that works deeply in one city beats one that works thinly in twenty.

Our story

Where the idea came from

ShelfLeap started from a repeated observation: a founder spends eighteen months perfecting a product, then spends the next eighteen trying to get one store to try it. Meanwhile, the store owner two kilometres away is asked to stock unfamiliar products every week by people he has no way to evaluate — so he says no to almost all of them.

Both sides are behaving rationally. The brand can't prove itself without shelf access, and the retailer can't risk shelf space on an unproven brand. It's a classic trust deadlock, and neither party can break it alone.

ShelfLeap breaks it by inserting a low-cost trial step that didn't exist before — a verified, curated, pooled sampling box — and by turning the retailer's reaction into structured data the brand can actually act on.

Not yet

What we're deliberately not building

Scope discipline is a feature. None of this gets built until the manual loop is proven in one city.

AI recommendation engine

Rules and manual approval first. Automate only what we've seen work by hand.

Live payment gateway

Test checkout during the prototype. Split-settlement rails come once order volume is real.

Complex logistics API

Box status is tracked manually through four stages until the volume justifies integration.

Mobile app

A responsive web app reaches every retailer with a phone. Native comes later, if at all.

Advanced analytics

Six honest metrics beat a dashboard of sixty nobody reads.

Nationwide marketplace

One city, deeply. Geographic expansion only after the loop is repeatable.
